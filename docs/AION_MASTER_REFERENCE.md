## 1. Evidence and status convention

Every material statement in this document is classified as follows:

- **CONFIRMED** — directly supported by repository content or another identified, current authoritative record reviewed for this phase. Confirmation applies only to the exact statement and evidence cited; it is not a blanket operational-readiness claim.
- **HISTORICAL** — recorded in earlier project notes, prior reports, conversations, or older repository documentation. It may be stale and must be rechecked before operational use.
- **PLANNED** — founder-approved identity, strategy, target architecture, product concept, or intended capability. It is not evidence of implementation or availability.
- **UNKNOWN** — not established by evidence reviewed for this phase.

A URL, repository file, UI label, product name, configuration variable, test fixture, workflow, or design document does not by itself prove ownership, production availability, legal registration, customer readiness, successful payment, revenue, custody, or an external integration.

## 6. Payments, commerce, and revenue controls

### 6.1 Required transaction lifecycle

1. Publish a clear service description, scope, final price, and currency.
2. Create a unique order identifier and persist its state.
3. Redirect the customer to the approved payment provider; do not handle raw card data unless a compliant provider-hosted flow is used.
4. Verify provider webhook signatures and reconcile merchant, order, amount, and currency.
5. Change order state only from authoritative provider evidence, not browser return parameters.
6. Make webhook processing idempotent; repeated events must not double-count revenue or duplicate delivery.
7. Release the purchased deliverable only after payment verification and applicable fulfillment checks.
8. Issue an accurate receipt and maintain refund, dispute, and reconciliation records.
9. Separate pending, failed, disputed, refunded, and settled amounts from confirmed revenue.

## 7. Security, privacy, legal, and financial governance

- Keep API keys, passwords, signing keys, seed phrases, certificates, recovery codes, and provider secrets out of source files, public issues, logs, and chat.
- Use least-privilege identities, protected branches, secret stores, MFA, and auditable access.
- Do not expose administrative tokens or provider credentials to browser code.
- Do not accept sensitive legal/customer files until security and privacy controls are independently tested.
- Define retention, deletion, backup, restore, and incident-response procedures.
- Do not move money, hold customer assets, deploy to mainnet, list tokens, or enable live trading without separate explicit authorization and applicable legal/security gates.
- Do not claim company registration, trademark ownership, regulatory approval, professional licensing, account ownership, or physical headquarters without authoritative records.
- Do not create financial commitments without explicit founder approval.

## 8. Sales, partnerships, and opportunity workflow

**PLANNED:** Use evidence-led prospecting, organic discovery, transparent offers, qualified outreach, and customer follow-up. Avoid paid advertising unless specifically approved.

Opportunity stages:

1. Research — discover a potential opportunity.
2. Verified Process — verify source, current status, eligibility, and process.
3. Due Diligence — assess legal, financial, technical, and operational risks.
4. Bid Ready — confirm authority, funding, and approval.
5. Acquired — only after binding completion and evidence of ownership.
6. Integrated & Monetized — only after lawful integration and verified operating results.

A lead, listing, public notice, draft offer, or research report is not an acquisition, contract, customer, or revenue event.
