# AION Space Commerce & Satellite Capacity Exchange — Production Strategy

## 1. Commercial model

AION will operate as a digital B2B space-services marketplace and qualified referral/broker layer.

- Supply side: satellite operators, capacity owners, Earth-observation providers, hosted-payload operators, ground-segment providers, and authorized distributors.
- Demand side: telecoms, maritime, aviation, energy, mining, agriculture, insurance, mapping, infrastructure, disaster response, enterprise connectivity, research, and other lawful commercial users.
- Revenue model: target a 5% success fee on qualifying contracts introduced or materially facilitated by AION.
- The commercial agreement must define whether the 5% is paid by the supplier, customer, or shared, and must cover renewals where commercially agreed.
- No satellite is represented as available until AION verifies the provider, service, coverage, licensing/authorization, technical parameters, and contractual authority.
- Government, defense, dual-use, export-controlled, or regulated transactions require appropriate authorization and compliance review before execution.

## 2. Product: AION Orbital Capacity Exchange

AION should not merely publish a directory. The product should create a machine-readable matching layer:

1. Provider publishes verified capacity/service inventory.
2. AION normalizes orbit, coverage, frequency/band, throughput, latency, availability window, SLA, geography, price basis, lead time, licensing constraints and permitted use.
3. Customer submits requirements.
4. AION's agent mesh scores compatible offers.
5. AION generates a transparent shortlist and commercial request.
6. Provider confirms availability and commercial terms.
7. AION records the introduction, contract, commission basis and audit trail.
8. Treasury Intelligence records commission receivable and settlement status; it does not move bank funds autonomously.

## 3. Differentiation

The defensible technical direction is not "an AI marketplace" by itself. AION should build a technical orchestration layer that:

- converts heterogeneous satellite/capacity inventories into a common capability graph;
- continuously matches customer requirements to multi-orbit/multi-provider supply;
- optimizes for coverage, time window, latency, reliability, price, SLA and compliance constraints;
- produces an auditable reason for every match;
- maintains cryptographically verifiable offer, quote, acceptance and commission records;
- prevents unauthorized tasking or disclosure of sensitive customer requirements;
- uses the AION Agent Mesh to decompose qualification, technical matching, compliance checks, commercial comparison and post-contract monitoring into bounded workflows.

This is a candidate invention direction, not a claim that patentability has been established.

## 4. Commercial launch sequence

Phase A — provider acquisition:
- Build a verified provider registry.
- Approach operators and authorized distributors with a non-exclusive referral/marketplace partnership.
- Use a standard 5% success-fee agreement subject to jurisdiction and licensing review.

Phase B — buyer acquisition:
- Build vertical landing pages and qualified inbound forms.
- Prioritize high-value use cases: remote enterprise connectivity, maritime, aviation, energy, mining, agriculture, insurance, infrastructure and emergency resilience.

Phase C — transaction layer:
- RFQ → shortlist → provider response → customer acceptance → contract evidence → commission invoice/receivable → settlement reconciliation.

## 5. Initial provider targets

Research-backed examples for outreach and partnership qualification include SES, Eutelsat/OneWeb, Viasat/Inmarsat, Planet, ICEYE and other authorized providers/distributors. These are targets for business development, not statements that they have agreed to AION terms.

## 6. Important legal boundary

AION must not claim to be a satellite operator, reseller, licensed telecom provider, broker, or government contractor unless the required authorization exists. The marketplace/referral role should be defined contractually for each jurisdiction and service.
